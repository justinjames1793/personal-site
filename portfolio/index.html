CREDE, a development advisory and construction project management firm, sends clients a quarterly market report that answers one question: should I build now? Putting each report together was a manual process that took more than 100 hours. I'm automating it on a repeatable data foundation. I traced every figure in the report to its original publisher, restructured it around the client's question, and built a catalog of 300+ data series that records where each one comes from, how to pull it, how often it's released and exactly what it measures. That catalog is the spec for an Azure platform I co-designed with a fellow intern, approved by the CEO in October. The goal is full automation: an AI drafts each report from the latest data and the firm's past reports, and an employee reviews and approves it before it goes to clients.

Decisions I made

  • Two releases a quarter. Market and cost data come out 2–4 weeks after quarter end, but sector data takes 3–7 weeks. Splitting the report into Current Conditions and Sector Forecast means neither waits for the other, and gives the firm two client touchpoints a quarter instead of one, a win for the marketing team as well as for readers. The CEO approved it in October.
  • Publish our own sector forecast. Many sector forecasts came from firms that compete with CREDE. I designed two methods side by side: build it from supply, demand and price inputs, or take the median and range of other publishers' year-over-year changes.
  • Compare like with like. Each series records what it measures and whose price it is (factory, contractor or retail). Sources are only combined when both match, and only by year-over-year change, never by level.
  • Azure over an all-Power-Platform build. Co-designed with a fellow intern. I compared three options on cost, testing and upkeep. At Microsoft list prices, the Azure option runs about $9–15 a month today, while Dataverse storage costs about $40 per GB a month once the included allowance is used up.

The problem

During a live UFC card, links to illegal streams spread through Reddit threads and Telegram channels, and most are gone within hours. Rights holders need to find them early and prove what they are. Searching by hand can't keep up, and most shared links are noise: news, highlights, betting promos and spam.

What we built

A pipeline of six Docker services around one PostgreSQL database. Scrapers collect every link shared on Reddit and in 68 Telegram channels. Every two minutes, an investigation engine reads the conversation around each new link and scores it 0 to 100 for urgency. Sixteen headless Chromium crawlers then claim links highest-urgency first, follow them up to two levels deep, and check the page for twelve piracy signals, saving the full click path as evidence.

My role

I was Data Lead on a five-person team.

  • Designed the PostgreSQL schema. One data model for every source: posts and comments are tagged with source and channel, so adding a platform means writing a scraper, not changing the ranker. A timestamp on each comment lets the system re-score a link whenever new replies arrive.
  • Built the Telegram ingestion. A Telethon client that polls public channels, pulls the first external link out of each message, and writes it into the shared schema with duplicate-safe batch inserts.
  • Co-designed the urgency score. Intent words in the surrounding conversation ("mirror", "still up"), how many people share the link, engagement, event keywords in the post title and words in the URL itself, minus points for words like "highlights" or "odds". A second score ignores popularity so brand-new links still get checked.
Technical details

Engineering highlights

  • Crawlers claim work with FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED and a 90-second lease, so no two grab the same link and no Redis is needed.
  • Piracy pages often flash the stream and then redirect, so detection runs every 500 ms during page load and inspects every pop-up.
  • Video alone proves nothing, so "soft" video signals only count once a "hard" signal fires, such as a piracy ad network or a "Server 1 / Server 2" picker.
  • One SPORT setting switches channels, keywords and allowlists between UFC and the World Cup.

Apr 2026 · UC San Diego DataFest

Where New Clinics Would Do the Most Good

2nd place, Best Use of External Data

In one weekend, my team of four analyzed 7.67M patient encounters from a regional health system. We grouped chronic patients by how their care unfolded over time, then used U.S. Census data to map the neighborhoods with the most risk.

How we did it
  • Cleaning. Joined six tables into an 8.1M-row master table, then kept about 701,000 chronic-care encounters.
  • Clustering. K-Means on 27-feature patient profiles sorted about 247K patients into 5 care-journey types, each with a 0 to 1 risk score.
  • Mapping. Linked patients to census block groups and mapped risk and patients per capita.

Risk was concentrated in specific neighborhoods around Topeka, the best targets for new clinics or outreach.

Team: Aditya Kalita, Ben Hashim, Ishaan Tibdewal and me.
